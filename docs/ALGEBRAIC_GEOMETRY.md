# Finite exact examples in affine algebraic geometry

This module connects polynomial equations to exact rational points and ideals.
It is **not** a general algebraic variety solver: the mathematical zero locus
can be infinite or contain irrational and complex points. All computations
use the rational coefficient field `Q` and bounded objects.

## The basic geometric object

For polynomials (f_1,\dots,f_s\in\mathbb{Q}[x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}]), the affine
zero locus over a chosen field extension (K) is

\[
V_K(f_1,\dots,f_s)=\{p\in K^n:f_i(p)=0\ \text{for every }i\}.
\]

`AffineSystem.contains(point)` checks this condition for **one exact rational
point**. `points_on_grid(axes)` enumerates only a prescribed finite Cartesian
product of rational coordinates. Empty output means that no supplied point
worked; it is **not** evidence of an empty real, complex, or rational variety.

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from calc.affine_geometry import AffineSystem
from calc.grobner import variables

x, y = variables(2)
circle = AffineSystem((x*x + y*y - 1,))
assert circle.contains((Fraction(3, 5), Fraction(4, 5)))
assert circle.points_on_grid(((-1, 0, 1), (-1, 0, 1))) == (
    (Fraction(-1), Fraction(0)),
    (Fraction(0), Fraction(-1)),
    (Fraction(0), Fraction(1)),
    (Fraction(1), Fraction(0)),
)
```

The example does not enumerate the complete circle; it only checks nine
integer candidate pairs.

## Ideal membership versus equality of zero loci

The ideal generated by the defining equations is

\[
I=(f_1,\dots,f_s)
 =\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{s}h_i f_i:h_i\in\mathbb{Q}[x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}]\right\}.
\]

`AffineSystem.ideal_contains(g)` uses the existing bounded Buchberger
Gröbner-basis implementation and computes the normal form of (g). When a
completed basis is returned, zero remainder decides membership in (I) over
the rational polynomial ring. A complexity-budget violation raises a visible
`ResourceLimitError`, not an incomplete negative answer.

```python
x, = variables(1)
double_origin = AffineSystem((x*x,))
assert double_origin.contains((0,))
assert double_origin.ideal_contains(x**3)
assert not double_origin.ideal_contains(x)
```

Both (x) and (x^2) vanish at zero, but (x\notin(x^2)). Membership in the
ideal is **not** equivalent to vanishing on its zero set. Radical ideals and
the Hilbert Nullstellensatz involve additional theory beyond this API.

## Exact local derivative information

`partial_derivative(polynomial, variable)` differentiates sparse rational
monomials formally. `AffineSystem.jacobian_at(point)` evaluates the matrix
\(\partial f_i/\partial x_j\) at a rational point, with no floating error.

```python
x, y = variables(2)
cusp = AffineSystem((y*y - x**3,))
assert cusp.jacobian_at((0, 0)) == ((Fraction(0), Fraction(0)),)
assert cusp.jacobian_at((1, 1)) == ((Fraction(-3), Fraction(2)),)
```

The Jacobian can help study a given point, but the returned matrix alone is
**not** a general dimension, smoothness, or singularity certificate for all
varieties; its interpretation requires additional hypotheses.

## Resource and input contract

- Ambient dimension: **1–4** variables.
- Defining equations: at most **16**, totaling at most **1,024 terms**.
- Polynomial total degree: at most **32** within an affine system.
- Rational coordinate size: at most **512 bits** per numerator/denominator.
- Grid candidates: up to **64** per axis and **10,000** Cartesian points total.
- Gröbner computations: bounded to 500 Buchberger pairs, 5,000 division
  steps, at most 32 basis elements and 1,024 terms.
- Exact results have an additional bit budget; overflowing it raises
  `ResourceLimitError` rather than approximating silently.
- Inputs must be `int`, `Fraction`, and `calc.grobner.MultiPolynomial` objects,
  never implicit floating-point coefficients or coordinates.

The component does not solve arbitrary multivariate equations, perform
primary decomposition, compute dimensions or coordinate rings, establish
irreducibility, or enumerate algebraic-closure points.
